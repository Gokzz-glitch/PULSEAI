Section 1: Pain Points & Core Understanding 🔎 
Exact Problem Definition 
The central objective of this initiative requires the engineering and deployment of an artificial 
intelligence-driven ecosystem capable of continuously analyzing real-time electrocardiogram 
(ECG) signals from affordable wearable sensors to accurately detect transient cardiac 
arrhythmias, seamlessly alerting patients and integrating with healthcare providers' digital 
infrastructures. 
Epidemiological and Ecosystem Context The cardiovascular disease (CVD) burden in India 
has reached epidemic proportions, representing the leading cause of mortality across the 
subcontinent. Current epidemiological data indicates that a quarter of all mortality in India is 
attributable to CVD, with an age-standardized death rate of 272 per 100,000 population, a 
figure that significantly eclipses the global average of 235 per 100,000.1 Extrapolating global 
prevalence rates to the Indian demographic indicates that approximately 6 million individuals 
are living with some form of cardiac arrhythmia, a condition characterized by irregularities in 
the rate or rhythm of the heartbeat.2 Most critically, sudden cardiac death (SCD) accounts for 
10% to 17% of overall mortality in India, often striking individuals with undiagnosed structural 
heart diseases or transient rhythm disorders such as non-sustained ventricular tachycardia.2 
Furthermore, community knowledge regarding emergency responses to these events remains 
critically low; studies show that while 65.5% of the population recognize coronary artery 
disease as a major risk factor, only 38.5% understand how to use an automated external 
defibrillator (AED), and merely 11.5% possess formal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) 
training.4 
In the specific context of Tamil Nadu and its capital, Chennai, the healthcare landscape 
presents a unique dichotomy. Chennai is a globally recognized hub for advanced medical 
tourism and cardiac care, boasting state-of-the-art facilities like Kauvery Hospital, which 
recently introduced advanced 3D electroanatomical mapping for complex arrhythmias 5, and 
Dr. Mehta's Hospitals, a multi-specialty institution with a 92-year legacy in cardiac care.7 The 
city attracts a vast influx of international and domestic medical travelers seeking high-quality, 
cost-effective cardiovascular treatments.8 Concurrently, the state government has initiated 
aggressive preventative healthcare measures to address the population-level disease burden. 
Programs like the Makkalai Thedi Maruthuvam (MTM) door-to-door screening scheme have 
revolutionized early detection for non-communicable diseases, achieving massive scale by 
screening millions for conditions like diabetes and hypertension at their doorsteps.11 
Additionally, the World Bank-supported Tamil Nadu Health System Reform Program (TNHSRP) 
actively targets improvements in the quality of care, aiming to strengthen the management of 
non-communicable diseases and reduce healthcare inequities.13 
However, despite these advanced tertiary centers and robust public health initiatives, a critical 
diagnostic gap remains. Transient arrhythmias, such as paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (AFib), 
frequently evade detection during standard clinical visits.2 A patient experiencing intermittent 
palpitations or pre-syncope may present a perfectly normal 12-lead ECG during a brief hospital 
consultation. This diagnostic disconnect leaves patients in a perpetual loop of uncertainty, 
facing the prohibitive costs and operational bottlenecks of traditional 24-hour Holter 
monitoring, which often fails to capture arrhythmic events that occur outside the brief 
monitoring window.2 
Root Causes 
The persistence of this diagnostic gap can be traced to several systemic and technological root 
causes: 
1.​ Transient Nature of Arrhythmias: Rhythm abnormalities like paroxysmal AFib or transient 
sinoatrial blocks are inherently episodic. They do not manifest continuously, meaning 
standard, short-duration clinical 12-lead ECG assessments frequently yield false-negative 
results.2 Research demonstrates that a 14-day continuous monitoring period yields a 
59.5% arrhythmia detection rate, compared to merely 19.0% for a standard 24-hour Holter, 
highlighting the necessity of long-term observation.15 
2.​ Resource Constraints and Workflow Inefficiencies: Traditional Holter monitors are 
cumbersome. They require complex manual setup involving multiple wet electrodes and 
wires, taking 15–20 minutes of a technician's time per patient. Subsequently, data retrieval 
requires the physical return of the device, followed by manual clinician review of the 
tracing, which severely limits scalability in resource-constrained public health settings and 
delays clinical intervention.16 
3.​ High Signal Noise in Ambulatory Settings: Modern wearable sensors, particularly those 
utilizing dry electrodes, are highly susceptible to motion artifacts (MA) and baseline 
wander caused by patient movement, respiration, and muscle tremors. Traditional digital 
signal processing algorithms (like the Pan-Tompkins algorithm) often fail to adequately 
filter this noise, leading to high false-positive rates that contribute to alarm fatigue among 
healthcare providers.17 
4.​ Diagnostic Disconnect and Data Silos: Data collected by fragmented, consumer-grade 
digital health devices often remains trapped in proprietary applications. This data fails to 
integrate into the national healthcare infrastructure, specifically India's Ayushman Bharat 
Digital Mission (ABDM), preventing the establishment of longitudinal patient records and 
hindering seamless data sharing between primary care physicians and specialist 
electrophysiologists.21

2b. Innovation Opportunity 
The competitive analysis reveals significant strategic whitespace for a solution tailored 
specifically to the Indian healthcare paradigm. The innovation opportunity lies at the 
intersection of extreme affordability, robust algorithmic noise handling, and seamless 
integration with the nation's digital public infrastructure. 
●​ Technical Differentiation (Edge AI & Advanced Motion Artifact Suppression): 
Traditional signal processing algorithms, such as the widely used Pan-Tompkins method, 
suffer dramatic performance degradation under the severe noise conditions common in 
wearable devices during physical activity.17 Hackathon teams can achieve massive 
differentiation by implementing advanced deep learning architectures designed 
specifically for noise resilience. For instance, the Deep Residual U-Net (ResU-Net) 
architecture has demonstrated exceptional capabilities in robust R-peak detection and 
morphological feature extraction even at extremely low signal-to-noise ratios (-6 dB), 
effectively mitigating the motion artifacts that plague continuous wearable monitoring.18 
Similarly, the adoption of Hybrid CNN-Transformer models allows the system to leverage 
convolutional layers for local feature extraction while utilizing transformer attention 
mechanisms to capture long-term dependencies in the ECG signal, improving 
classification robustness across different patients.19 Furthermore, executing quantized 
versions of these models directly on the edge (e.g., using TinyML frameworks on an ESP32 
microcontroller) significantly reduces cloud computing latency and eliminates the 
dependency on constant mobile internet connectivity—a crucial factor for rural 
deployment in Tamil Nadu.38 
●​ Process Innovation (Native ABDM & FHIR Integration): A major limitation of current 
competitors is that they trap valuable patient data within proprietary corporate silos. An 
innovative, future-proof solution will natively integrate with India's Ayushman Bharat Digital 
Mission (ABDM), the government's flagship initiative to build an integrated digital health 
infrastructure.21 By structuring the AI's diagnostic output not as a proprietary PDF, but as 
standard HL7 FHIR R4 DiagnosticReport resources 25, the wearable application can 
function as a Health Information Provider (HIP). This allows the application to push 
clinical-grade, interoperable data directly into the patient's Ayushman Bharat Health 
Account (ABHA). This process innovation enables cardiologists at tertiary referral centers 
to instantly pull longitudinal records seamlessly, fulfilling the vision of ABDM to ensure 
affordability and accessibility via the Health Information Exchange and Consent Manager 
(HIE-CM).22 
●​ Feature Gaps (Cultural and Systemic Adaptation): A winning solution will specifically 
align with state-level initiatives. By designing the mobile interface with localization in mind 
(e.g., offering Tamil language support) and creating a specialized administrative dashboard 
tailored for field health workers (such as the Village Health Nurses operating the Makkalai 
Thedi Maruthuvam scheme) 11, the solution fundamentally pivots from being a mere 
consumer health gadget to becoming a highly scalable, enterprise-grade public health 
tool. 
●​ User Experience Improvements (Actionable Clinical Triaging): Instead of overwhelming 
the patient with complex ECG waveforms they cannot interpret, the mobile application 
should act as a triage engine. When the AI detects an arrhythmia, it should correlate the 
event with user-inputted symptoms (e.g., prompting the user: "We detected an irregular 
rhythm. Are you feeling dizzy or experiencing chest pain?"). This contextual data provides 
invaluable clinical insights to the attending physician, moving the system from passive data 
collection to active clinical decision support. 
Section 3: Feasibility of Execution ⚙️ 
Engineering a fully integrated hardware, software, and AI solution within a constrained 
hackathon timeframe (typically 3 days, as seen in standard health tech events 43) is highly 
ambitious. However, it is entirely feasible if teams strategically avoid "reinventing the wheel" by 
leveraging off-the-shelf electronic components, pre-trained neural network weights, and 
established open-source Internet of Things (IoT) cloud platforms. 
Timeline Assessment: Yes, a working prototype is feasible within a 72-hour window. The 
hardware assembly is straightforward and documented extensively in the maker community. 
The primary time investment must be directed toward configuring the data telemetry pipeline 
and fine-tuning the AI inference API, rather than attempting to train complex models from 
scratch. 
Technical Requirements & Stack Architecture: 
1.​ Hardware Layer (Signal Acquisition): The integration of an AD8232 Single-Lead Heart 
Rate Monitor Front End module with an ESP32 Microcontroller provides an optimal, 
highly documented hardware baseline for ambulatory ECG acquisition. The AD8232 is 
specifically designed to extract, amplify, and filter small biopotential signals in the 
presence of noisy conditions, such as those created by motion or remote electrode 
placement.39 This hardware combination is exceptionally affordable, costing under ₹2,500 
(approximately $30 USD), and can be assembled and programmed using the Arduino IDE 
in under an hour.39 The ESP32 handles the analog-to-digital conversion and utilizes its 
built-in Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) or Wi-Fi capabilities for seamless data transmission to 
the mobile application or directly to the cloud.45 
2.​ Telemetry & Cloud Layer (Data Routing): Building a custom, secure backend from 
scratch during a hackathon is an anti-pattern that consumes critical development hours. 
Instead, teams should utilize established IoT platforms like Ubidots.46 By utilizing the 
lightweight MQTT messaging protocol, the ESP32 can publish the continuous ECG stream 
to the Ubidots broker, which provides instant data visualization capabilities and secure API 
endpoints that the AI inference engine can consume.47 
3.​ AI Inference Layer (Classification Engine): Training a deep neural network on millions of 
ECG parameters from scratch requires significant GPU compute and days of processing 
time. Teams should leverage transfer learning by utilizing open-source repositories (such 
as the awni/ecg model 32 or robust ResU-Net implementations 18) that have been 
pre-trained on datasets like the PhysioNet MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database.34 The hackathon 
focus should remain on building a robust inference pipeline—deploying the pre-trained 
model via a framework like FastAPI or Flask, which ingests the real-time data stream, 
executes the classification, and returns the diagnostic result.33 
Data Availability: Extensive, high-quality training data is freely available for teams prior to the 
event. The PhysioNet platform hosts the seminal MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, which contains 
thousands of annotated beats.34 For teams seeking to implement more robust noise-handling 
algorithms, the MIT-BIH Noise Stress Test Database provides essential data containing 
calibrated amounts of baseline wander, muscle artifacts, and electrode motion artifacts.36 
Additionally, the Chapman University dataset provides a massive repository of 45,152 patient 
records for comprehensive model training.3

MVP Definition for the Hackathon: 
A highly competitive Minimum Viable Product (MVP) consists of the AD8232 sensor wired to 
the ESP32 microcontroller, continuously streaming single-lead ECG data via Bluetooth or Wi-Fi 
to a paired Android application. The application routes this data to a cloud-hosted Python 
inference engine utilizing a pre-trained CNN or Transformer model. Upon identifying an 
irregular RR-interval or specific arrhythmic morphology (e.g., the absence of a P-wave 
indicative of AFib), the cloud immediately triggers an alert back to the mobile app. Crucially, the 
app then formats this anomaly event as an HL7 FHIR JSON payload, demonstrating simulated 
readiness to push the data directly into a patient's Ayushman Bharat Health Account (ABHA).

Section 5: Clarity of Problem Statement 🧩 
To execute flawlessly and maximize evaluation scores, teams must establish rigid, clearly 
defined boundaries regarding what the solution will and will not attempt to achieve within the 
confines of the hackathon framework. Ambiguity is the primary cause of project failure in 
time-constrained environments. 
Potential Misinterpretations & Ambiguities: 
●​ The "Classification" Trap: A common pitfall for technically focused teams is treating this 
purely as a Kaggle-style data science challenge. They may spend 90% of the hackathon 
attempting to maximize F1-scores on the pristine MIT-BIH dataset 34, while completely 
ignoring the severe reality of hardware-induced motion artifacts, baseline wander, and 
real-world data transmission protocols.19 Evaluators look for end-to-end clinical systems, 
not just highly optimized algorithms operating in a vacuum. 
●​ The "12-Lead" Fallacy: Teams often overpromise by attempting to diagnose complex 
structural heart diseases, such as ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), 
using data from a single-lead wearable. While single-lead telemetry is highly effective for 
identifying rhythm analysis and timing anomalies (arrhythmias), it is clinically inadequate 
for the comprehensive morphological diagnosis of ischemic heart disease, which requires 
spatial evaluation across multiple axes of the heart.59 Teams must explicitly scope their 
claims strictly to rhythm abnormalities to maintain clinical credibility.

Section 6: Evaluator's Perspective 🎯 
Understanding the psychological and professional framework of the evaluation panel is critical 
for strategic positioning. Evaluators in health tech hackathons are typically a mix of clinicians, 
regulatory experts, and technical architects. 
Evaluation Criteria Priority: 
1.​ Feasibility & Clinical Safety (30%): Does the team exhibit a profound understanding of 
the clinical limitations of single-lead ECGs?59 Have they accounted for real-world motion 
artifacts, or are they relying on perfectly clean, unrealistic laboratory data? 
2.​ Impact & Market Fit (25%): Is the hardware solution economically viable for the Indian 
demographic? Does the software ecosystem align seamlessly with state health programs 
(e.g., the World Bank-funded TNHSRP)?13 
3.​ Technical Completeness (20%): Does the data pipeline flow logically and securely from 
the hardware sensor to the cloud inference engine and back to the mobile UI without 
relying on major "Wizard of Oz" mockups? 
4.​ Regulatory & Interoperability Awareness (15%): This is the ultimate differentiator. Has 
the team proactively addressed the rigorous mandates of the Digital Personal Data 
Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 51 and integrated ABDM/FHIR semantic standards?21 
5.​ Uniqueness (10%): Has the team moved beyond rudimentary, decades-old signal 
processing algorithms (like Pan-Tompkins) into modern, robust deep learning 
architectures?18 
Scoring Red Flags ⚠️: 
●​ Ignoring Data Privacy Legislation: Failing to implement transparent consent mechanisms 
in the application UI directly violates the core tenets of the newly enacted DPDP Act 
2023.51 Evaluators with healthcare administration experience will instantly penalize 
platforms that carelessly ingest and transmit sensitive health data without documenting 
verifiable consent or outlining a 72-hour breach notification protocol.52 
●​ Unrealistic Clinical Claims: Emphatically claiming that the single-lead device can reliably 
detect ischemic heart disease, or positioning it as a complete replacement for a 
comprehensive 12-lead clinical ECG.59 This demonstrates a dangerous lack of domain 
expertise. 
●​ Black-Box AI without Explainability: Physicians require interpretability to trust 
algorithms. If the AI simply outputs "Arrhythmia Detected" without highlighting the specific 
anomalous segment of the ECG strip (e.g., utilizing attention mechanism heatmaps or 
saliency maps 37), clinical adoption will face insurmountable friction. 
Winning Angle: Teams must position the solution not as a commercial product, but as a 
"Digital Public Good for Cardiac Health." Frame the narrative around bridging the critical gap 
between India's advanced, rapidly expanding digital infrastructure (ABDM and ABHA accounts 
22) and grassroots healthcare delivery mechanisms (Makkalai Thedi Maruthuvam 11). Emphasize 
that while existing competitors focus on building expensive, siloed consumer gadgets for the 
elite, your team is architecting an ultra-affordable, highly interoperable clinical triage engine. 
Stress that the platform is fundamentally compliant with Indian regulatory hardware 
frameworks (CDSCO) 48 and cutting-edge data privacy laws (DPDP) 51, making it uniquely ready

Section 8: Antigravity Execution Plan 🚀 
Provide this comprehensive, detailed engineering spec to your Antigravity agent. It dictates the 
exact technical constraints, libraries, compliance measures, and AI architectures required to 
build the final, evaluator-ready product. 
Project MVP Scope: Build an AI-driven, single-lead ECG analysis pipeline linking an ESP32 
hardware device to a predictive cloud model, compliant with Indian digital health standards 
(ABDM) and the 2023 Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act. 
Phase 1: Edge Hardware & Telemetry Pipeline 
●​ Hardware Baseline: The system utilizes the AD8232 Single-Lead Heart Rate Monitor 
Front End connected to an ESP32 Microcontroller.45 This ensures the BOM (Bill of 
Materials) remains highly affordable (under ₹2,500 / $30).45 
●​ Data Acquisition: Configure the ESP32 to perform high-speed analog signal acquisition at 
an optimal rate of 500 samples per second. 
●​ Data Transmission Protocol: Avoid heavy HTTP requests. Utilize lightweight MQTT 
messaging. 
○​ Action for Antigravity: Write the C++/Arduino sketch to read from the ESP32 ADC 
pins. Implement the MQTT client (e.g., PubSubClient) to continuously publish the raw 
physiological data payload via Wi-Fi to an IoT broker like Ubidots or a custom AWS IoT 
Core instance. 
Phase 2: Signal Preprocessing & Noise Denoising 
Raw ambulatory ECG data is notoriously corrupted by motion artifacts (MA), electromyogram 
(EMG) noise, and baseline wander. 
●​ Denoising Library Implementation: 
○​ Action for Antigravity: In the Python backend, import and utilize the open-source 
SigClean Python library. 
○​ Use SigClean to apply a multi-stage filtering process: first, apply a high-pass filter to 
remove baseline wander; second, apply a notch filter to remove powerline 
interference (50Hz in India); third, utilize its artifact removal toolkit to suppress severe 
EMG motion artifacts before feeding the data to the classifier. 
Phase 3: AI Inference Architecture (HCTG-Net) 
Traditional machine learning and simple 1D-CNNs (like Pan-Tompkins algorithms) fail under high 
ambulatory noise because they rely heavily on fragile R-peak detection. 
●​ Advanced Model Selection: Implement a Hybrid CNN-Transformer Network 
(HCTG-Net). 
○​ Why: The CNN branch extracts localized morphological features (e.g., QRS complex 
shapes), while the Transformer attention mechanism captures long-range temporal 
dependencies across the heartbeat sequence. Crucially, this architecture does not 
rely on strict R-peak detection, making it robust to noisy wearable data. 
●​ Training & Benchmarking: 
○​ Action for Antigravity: Train the model using the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database 
(available via PhysioNet or Kaggle).34 Target the benchmark performance of ~99.46% 
accuracy and 0.9711 F1-score demonstrated by recent HCTG-Net implementations. 
○​ Expose the trained weights via a lightweight Python FastAPI inference endpoint that 
consumes the MQTT data stream from Phase 1. 
Phase 4: Interoperability & ABDM Integration Layer 
Proprietary data silos are a massive red flag. The solution must natively integrate with the 
Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) ecosystem. 
●​ Target: Milestone 2 (M2) Integration - Building Health Information Provider (HIP) services. 
●​ FHIR Standard Implementation: 
○​ Action for Antigravity: Format every positive arrhythmia detection event generated 
by the AI strictly as an HL7 FHIR R4 DiagnosticReport JSON resource.42 
○​ Ensure the JSON payload specifically incorporates the results tag, the conclusion tag 
(the AI's classification), and the presentedForm or media tag (to attach a visual plot of 
the anomalous ECG strip).42 
○​ Point the payload generation logic toward the ABDM Sandbox testing environment 
(https://sandbox.abdm.gov.in/) to demonstrate live interoperability testing. 
Phase 5: Mobile Application & DPDP Act 2023 
Compliance 
The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act of 2023 mandates strict rules for handling 
health data as a "Significant Data Fiduciary".52 
●​ Consent Engineering: 
○​ Action for Antigravity: Build the mobile UI (React Native/Flutter) with strict, verifiable 
consent mechanisms.53 Users must explicitly opt-in (via clear affirmative action) 
before any ECG data is collected, transmitted, or stored.55 
●​ Security & Access: 
○​ Implement Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).53 The database architecture must 
segregate patient-viewable data from the physician-facing triage alerts. 
○​ Implement end-to-end encryption for data in transit and at rest.53 
●​ Breach Readiness: 
○​ Architect a simulated backend logging system capable of generating a breach 
notification report within 72 hours to the Data Protection Board, a strict requirement 
under the DPDP Act rules.52 
Phase 6: CDSCO Regulatory Documentation 
Framework 
To prove commercial viability in India, the solution must align with the Central Drugs Standard 
Control Organisation (CDSCO) Medical Devices Rules, 2017.48 
●​ Action for Antigravity: Generate a mock technical documentation package within the 
project repository that includes: 
1.​ An Essential Principles Checklist demonstrating conformity with safety 
requirements for AI medical software.48 
2.​ Software version release certificates.48 
3.​ A basic Risk Analysis and Control document prioritizing the mitigation of false-positive 
arrhythmia alerts caused by wearable motion artifacts.48

Key Takeaways Box 
●​ 🎯 Problem Clarity: Traditional ambulatory ECG monitoring is prohibitively expensive, 
physically cumbersome, and structurally detached from India's centralized digital health 
infrastructure, leading to systemic missed diagnoses of life-threatening transient 
arrhythmias.2 
●​ 💡 Innovation Angle: Transitioning from siloed, proprietary hardware to a low-cost, 
edge-AI enabled wearable that aggressively suppresses severe motion artifacts via deep 
learning (ResU-Net or Transformers) 18 and natively outputs interoperable FHIR data for the 
Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission.21 
●​ ⚙️ MVP Scope: A highly functional, sub-₹2,500 hardware prototype combining an 
AD8232 sensor and ESP32 microcontroller 45, continuously streaming to a cloud-hosted

that is deeply embedded with DPDP Act-compliant user consent flows.51 
●​ 🌍 Impact Potential: Fundamentally democratizes cardiac care by serving as a highly 
scalable, low-cost screening tool for state-level initiatives like Tamil Nadu's Makkalai Thedi 
Maruthuvam 11, preempting ischemic stroke and sudden cardiac death 2 in 
resource-constrained rural populations. 
●​ ⚠️ Critical Blocker: The presence of severe motion artifacts (MA) during active 
ambulatory use can cripple machine learning accuracy 19; therefore, teams must prioritize 
sophisticated signal denoising pipelines alongside strict adherence to CDSCO regulatory 
frameworks for AI-enabled medical devices.48
